This chapter is your launchpad for integrating with the WEIR AI API. We'll cover the typical integration path, what you need before you begin, how to get your developer account set up, and the crucial steps for API access and authentication.

## 2.1 Overview of the Integration Workflow

Integrating with the WEIR AI API generally follows a clear path. Understanding this workflow will help you plan your development efforts.

### Your WEIR AI Integration Adventure: A Friendly Guide

Ready to dive into the WEIR AI API? Think of this as your friendly map. Integrating with us is a journey with a few key stops along the way. Let's walk through it together:

<Steps>
  <Step title="👋 Welcome Aboard! Getting You Set Up" icon="EnvelopeOpen">
    It all starts with an email invite – your golden ticket to join your organization on the WEIR AI platform. Just click the link, and we'll smoothly guide you through creating your developer account. Easy peasy!
  </Step>
  <Step title="🔑 Getting Your Keys to the Kingdom (API Access)" icon="Key">
    Next up, you'll need an API key for your company's AdTech platform. This little key is what lets your systems talk to ours. Once you have it, tuck it away safely in your development setup. We'll chat more about the best ways to do that in a bit.
  </Step>
  <Step title="🛠️ Building the Heart of Your Integration" icon="Code">
    - **Become an API Whiz:** Take a little time to explore this documentation. Get familiar with how our API endpoints work, the way we structure data, and how authentication keeps everything secure.
    - **Teach Your System to Say "Hello":** You'll need to make sure your API client (that's the part of your code that connects to our API) knows how to properly send the API key in its requests.
    - **Let the Scanning Begin!:** This is where the real fun starts. You'll build the feature in your system that sends ad creative details (like a URL to an image or video) over to our `POST /mvp/creatives/scan` endpoint.
  </Step>
  <Step title="🔍 Decoding the Results" icon="MagnifyingGlass">
    After a scan, our API will send back its findings. Your code will need to look at this response to see if any NIL Personas (that's Name, Image, Likeness) popped up and what their licensing story is. This involves a bit of JSON parsing, grabbing important IDs, and understanding any license terms.
  </Step>
  <Step title="🤝 Handling License Decisions (When They Come Up)" icon="Handshake">
    If a scan flags a NIL Persona, your platform or an advertiser might need to make a call – like accepting or disputing a license. When that happens, you'll use our `POST /mvp/license-interactions` endpoint to let us know what happened. It's all about sending us the right details, like the `scan_id`, `persona_id`, and the `interaction_type`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="⚙️ Keeping Everything Running Smoothly (Error Handling & Logging)" icon="Gear">
    Life happens, and sometimes things don't go exactly as planned. It's always a good idea to build in some solid error handling for your API calls. Think about what to do if there's a network hiccup, an authentication blip, or if a request isn't quite right. And trust us, good logging of your requests and responses will be your best friend when you need to troubleshoot or just keep an eye on how things are flowing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="🚀 Testing, Testing, 1-2-3... And Go Live!" icon="RocketLaunch">
    Before you introduce your shiny new integration to the world, give it a thorough test drive. Try out all sorts of scenarios: what happens when a NIL match is found? When it isn't? How does it handle different license types? What about those pesky errors? Once you're feeling confident and everything's looking good, it's time to hit the launch button and deploy your solution!
  </Step>
</Steps>

## 2.3 Developer Onboarding & Account Setup
<Note>This section relates to EPIC D1.</Note>

Your journey starts with becoming a recognized developer within your organization on the WEIR AI platform.

### 2.3.1 Understanding Roles: Platform Owner vs. Developer

Within the WEIR AI ecosystem, user roles define permissions and capabilities:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Platform Owner (e.g., Sheryl)" icon="UserCrown">
    Typically an administrator or manager responsible for:
    - Registering the company as an Organization on WEIR AI.
    - Registering specific AdTech platforms (e.g., "Yoyodyne DSP").
    - Managing API keys for these platforms.
    - Inviting team members, such as Developers.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Developer (e.g., Carlton)" icon="UserGear">
    This is your role. You are responsible for:
    - Integrating the WEIR AI API with your Organization's AdTech platforms.
    - Using the API key (provided by the Platform Owner).
    - Accessing API documentation and resources.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

### 2.3.2 Accepting an Organization Invitation
<Note>Corresponds to User Story D1.1</Note>

The onboarding process for a developer begins with an email invitation.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Receive Invitation" icon="Mailbox">
    Your Platform Owner will send an invitation to your email address. This email will contain a unique link to join their organization on WEIR AI.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click the Invitation Link" icon="CursorClick">
    Clicking this link will take you to a WEIR AI page to begin your account creation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete Account Creation" icon="UserPlus">
    - You'll be prompted to complete a simplified account creation form.
    - This typically involves confirming your email (which may be pre-filled), providing your full name, and setting a secure password.
    - The system uses an `invite_token` from the URL to associate you with the correct organization.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit Your Details" icon="PaperPlane">
    Upon submission, the WEIR AI platform will:
    - Validate the `invite_token`.
    - Create a new WEIR User account for you.
    - Automatically associate your user account with your Platform Owner's Organization and assign you the "Developer" role.
    - Mark the invitation token as 'accepted' or 'claimed'.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirmation" icon="CheckCircle">
    You should receive a confirmation (e.g., a success message on the web page) indicating your account has been created and you're now part of the organization. You may be logged in automatically.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info title="Behind the Scenes: Invitation Claim">
  The process of accepting an invitation and setting up an account is handled by the WEIR AI frontend/backend. For context, this typically involves a call to an endpoint like:

  `POST /mvp/organization-invitations/claim`

  **Purpose:** Allows an invited user to claim their invitation and set up their account.
  **Key Payload Fields:** `invite_token`, `full_name`, `password`, `password_confirmation`.

  (Full details of this endpoint are in the API Reference, Section 4.5.1. This is primarily for your understanding of the underlying mechanism.)
</Info>

## 2.4 API Access & Authentication

With your developer account set up, the next critical step is gaining access to and understanding how to use the API key for authentication.

### 2.4.1 Understanding API Keys: Purpose and Importance

An API Key is a unique secret token that you must include in your requests to the WEIR AI API. Its purposes are:

<CardGroup cols={1}>
  <Card title="Authentication" icon="Fingerprint">
    It verifies that the request is coming from a legitimate, authorized source (i.e., your organization's registered platform).
  </Card>
  <Card title="Authorization" icon="ShieldCheck">
    It determines what API functionalities your application has permission to access.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Usage Tracking/Billing" icon="ChartLine">
    It allows WEIR AI to track API usage for your organization's account.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Warning>
  **Treat your API Key like a password.** It grants significant access to WEIR AI services on behalf of your organization.
</Warning>

### 2.4.2 Obtaining Your API Key
<Note>Corresponds to User Story D1.2</Note>

For the MVP (Minimum Viable Product) stage, the API key for your AdTech platform (e.g., "Yoyodyne DSP") is typically generated by the Platform Owner (e.g., Sheryl) when they register the platform on WEIR AI.

- **Provision by Platform Owner:** Your Platform Owner will securely communicate the API key value to you (the Developer). This might be done through a secure internal channel.
- **Future UI Access (Potential):** In future iterations of the WEIR AI platform, there might be a user interface where Developers with appropriate permissions can view or copy the API key for platforms they are assigned to.

<Info title="System Context: API Key Generation">
  Conceptually, the Platform Owner might interact with an endpoint like the following (or a UI that uses it) to manage API keys:

  `GET /mvp/organizations/me/platforms/{platform_id}/api-keys`

  **Purpose:** Lists API key metadata (not the key itself after initial generation, for security reasons) for a specific platform.

  (Full details of this conceptual endpoint are in the API Reference, Section 4.5.2.)
</Info>

### 2.4.3 Securely Storing and Using Your API Key (Best Practices)

Once you have the API key, its security is paramount.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="🚨 DO NOTs for API Key Security" icon="XCircle" defaultOpen={true}>
    - **DO NOT** hardcode API keys directly into your application's source code. This is a major security risk, as keys can be accidentally exposed in version control systems or client-side code.
    - **DO NOT** share API keys insecurely. Avoid sending API keys via email, instant messaging, or other non-secure channels.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="✅ DOs for API Key Security" icon="CheckCircle" defaultOpen={true}>
    - **DO** use environment variables: Store the API key in an environment variable on your server or in your development environment. Your application code can then read the key from this environment variable.
    - **DO** use a secrets management system: For production environments, consider using a dedicated secrets management service (e.g., HashiCorp Vault, AWS Secrets Manager, Google Cloud Secret Manager, Azure Key Vault).
    - **DO** restrict access: Ensure that only authorized personnel and services have access to the API key.
    - **DO** be prepared to rotate keys: If an API key is compromised or if your organization's policy requires it, you should be prepared to rotate the key (i.e., generate a new one and update your applications).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Tip title="Authentication Method">
  The WEIR AI API typically expects the API key to be sent as a Bearer Token in the `Authorization` HTTP header of your requests.
  (More details in Section 4.1. Authentication).
  <CodeBlock>
    Authorization: Bearer YOUR_API_KEY
  </CodeBlock>
</Tip>

### 2.4.4 Making Your First Authenticated Call

Once you have your API key and have stored it securely, you're ready to make your first authenticated call to the WEIR AI API. This will confirm that your setup is correct and you can communicate with the API.

For the WEIR AI MVP, the primary functional endpoint you'll interact with is `POST /mvp/creatives/scan`. This is an excellent candidate for your first call.

<Steps>
  <Step title="1. Identify the Endpoint URL" icon="Link">
    This will be the Base URL (see Section 4.2) plus `/mvp/creatives/scan`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="2. Prepare the Request Body" icon="FileCode">
    You'll need to provide a JSON payload. For a simple test, this would include a `creative_url` pointing to a publicly accessible image.
    <CodeBlock lang="json">
{`{
  "creative_url": "https://example.com/path/to/your/test_creative.jpg"
}`}
    </CodeBlock>
  </Step>
  <Step title="3. Include the Authorization Header" icon="ShieldLock">
    Add the `Authorization` header with your API key.
    <CodeBlock>
      Authorization: Bearer YOUR_ACTUAL_API_KEY
    </CodeBlock>
  </Step>
  <Step title="4. Set the Content-Type Header" icon="FileText">
    Specify that you're sending JSON data.
    <CodeBlock>
      Content-Type: application/json
    </CodeBlock>
  </Step>
  <Step title="5. Make the POST Request" icon="PaperPlane">
    Use your preferred HTTP client or tool (e.g., cURL, Postman, or a library in your programming language) to send the request.
    <br />
    Example using cURL:
    <CodeBlock lang="bash">
{`curl -X POST \\
  'https://api.weir.ai/v1/scan' \\ # Replace with actual Base URL
  -H 'Authorization: Bearer YOUR_ACTUAL_API_KEY' \\
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \\
  -d '{
        "creative_url": "https://www.weir.ai/images/some-test-image.png"
      }'`}
    </CodeBlock>
  </Step>
  <Step title="6. Check the Expected Outcome" icon="CheckSquare">
    <AccordionGroup>
      <Accordion title="✅ Successful Call (HTTP 200 OK)" icon="CheckCircle">
        If your API key is valid and the request is well-formed, you should receive an HTTP 200 OK response. The response body will contain the scan results (either with NIL matches or an empty `matches` array if no Personas were detected).
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="❌ Authentication Error (HTTP 401 Unauthorized)" icon="WarningCircle">
        If your API key is missing, invalid, or expired, you'll likely receive an HTTP 401 Unauthorized error. Double-check your key and how you're including it in the header.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="⚠️ Other Errors (HTTP 4xx/5xx)" icon="XOctagon">
        You might receive other error codes for issues like malformed JSON (400 Bad Request) or server-side problems. Refer to Section 4.7 for error code details.
      </Accordion>
    </AccordionGroup>
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip icon="PartyPopper">
  Successfully making this first call means your authentication is working, and you're ready to proceed with deeper integration of the WEIR AI API's functionalities!
</Tip>
